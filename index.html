What Is Biomedical
Engineering?

Biomedical Engineering (BME) is a field that combines biology, medicine, engineering, and technology to solve problems related to the human body and healthcare. Biomedical engineers use science and engineering to design, create, test, and improve medical technologies that can help people.

01 / Cells

📖 What Is a
Cell?

A cell is the smallest unit of life that can carry out the basic processes needed for an organism to live. All living organisms are made of one or more cells.🧠 Two Main Types of Cells Prokaryotic cells Do not have a nucleus. Example: bacteria. Eukaryotic cells Have a nucleus. Examples: animal, plant, fungi, and protist cells. 🌱 Plant vs. 🐾 Animal Cells Plant cells Have: Cell wall Chloroplasts Large central vacuole Nucleus Cell membrane Cytoplasm Mitochondria Animal cells Have: Nucleus Cell membrane Cytoplasm Mitochondria Ribosomes Smaller vacuoles ⭐ Main difference: Plant cells have cell walls and chloroplasts; animal cells do not. ⚡ How Cells Get Energy Cells need energy to perform their activities. Food → cellular respiration → usable energy (ATP) The mitochondria are important sites of cellular respiration in eukaryotic cells. 🧬 Cells & DNA The nucleus of a eukaryotic cell contains most of the cell's DNA. DNA → genetic instructions → proteins → cell functions 🩺 Biomedical Engineering Connection Biomedical engineers study cells because cells are involved in: 🧬 Genetics 🧫 Disease research 🧪 Tissue engineering 💊 Drug-delivery technologies 🦴 Regenerative medicine 🔬 Medical research Understanding cells helps biomedical engineers understand how the human body works and how medical technologies can interact with biological systems.
